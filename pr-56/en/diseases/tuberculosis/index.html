Tuberculosis Category 3 notifiable disease

Tuberculosis spreads through the air; see a doctor if you have coughed for more than 2 weeks. Taking medicine regularly for at least 6 months cures almost everyone; contact screening and latent TB treatment prevent future disease.

What to do

If youhave coughed for more than 2 weeks

Get a chest X-ray at a chest clinic

If you have coughed for 2 weeks or more, have sputum or are losing weight, see a chest physician for an X-ray and sputum test.

If youare diagnosed with TB

Join DOTS and take medicine for at least 6 months

A DOTS worker accompanies you to take medicine every day; completing treatment cures almost everyone, while stopping early can cause drug resistance.

If youa family member has TB

Take part in contact screening

The health centre will arrange a chest X-ray and latent TB test, paid for by the government.

Symptoms and warning signs

Early pulmonary TB may cause few symptoms. Common signs are a cough lasting more than 2 weeks, sputum, chest pain, poor appetite, weight loss, fatigue, afternoon fever and night sweats; coughing blood may occur in severe cases.

TB can also affect lymph nodes, bones, kidneys and the meninges. People with latent TB infection carry the bacteria without symptoms and cannot infect others.

Warning signs — seek care immediately if any appear: Coughing blood, breathlessness, persistent high fever or rapid weight loss: seek care promptly.

How it spreads

TB spreads through the air. When a person with infectious pulmonary TB coughs, sneezes, talks or sings, droplets containing bacteria float in the air and are inhaled by others. Risk is highest for people who spend long periods with the patient in enclosed spaces, such as household members.

TB does not spread through shared utensils, handshakes or clothing. Infectiousness falls markedly after about 2 weeks of regular treatment.

Prevention

  • Early detection and complete treatment stop transmission.
  • Close contacts should take part in contact screening; those with latent TB infection are advised to take preventive treatment (such as 3HP, once weekly for 12 doses), which lowers the risk of disease by over 90%.
  • Infants receive BCG at 5 months (recommended 5–8 months) to prevent severe childhood TB such as TB meningitis.
  • Keep rooms ventilated and wear a mask when coughing.

Diagnosis and treatment

TB is treated with a combination of drugs for at least 6 months (four drugs for 2 months, then two drugs for 4 months); drug-resistant TB takes longer.

Taiwan runs DOTS (directly observed treatment): a worker brings the medicine daily and watches the patient take it, improving cure rates and preventing resistance. Treatment costs are covered by the government.

Vaccine

BCG is a routine government-funded vaccine given once at 5 months (recommended 5–8 months). It mainly prevents severe TB in young children and offers limited protection against adult pulmonary TB; adults do not need a booster.

Current situation and statistics

New TB cases in Taiwan have fallen steadily since 2005 towards the WHO 2035 elimination target; see the dataset for annual figures.

2035 消除結核

2035 年發生率降至每十萬人口 10 例以下；策略為早期發現、完整治療、潛伏感染治療。

高風險族群主動篩檢

依計畫對高風險族群定期胸部 X 光篩檢，並推動潛伏結核感染檢驗與治療。

Subsidies and services

Subsidies, applications and reporting services related to this disease.

  • 潛伏結核感染檢驗與治療
    結核病接觸者與計畫對象可接受公費 IGRA 檢驗與潛伏結核感染治療（3HP、1HP、3HR、4R、9H），治療期間由關懷員陪伴服藥（DOPT）。

    結核病個案就醫由健保給付、部分負擔由疾管署支應；多重抗藥性結核病由 TMTC 收治並由公費支應；依法施行隔離治療者，費用由政府負擔。
