Measles Category 2 notifiable disease

Measles is an extremely contagious viral disease that spreads through the air. Two doses of MMR vaccine prevent it effectively; people born in 1966 or later without immunity should be assessed before travelling.

What to do

If youhave a fever with a rash

Call ahead, wear a mask and see a doctor

Phone the clinic first, wear a mask, avoid public transport and tell the doctor about your travel and contact history.

If youwere told you had contact with a measles case

Monitor your health for 18 days

Self-monitor for 18 days from the contact; MMR within 72 hours of exposure may be considered.

If youwere born in 1966 or later and plan to travel

Get assessed 2–4 weeks before departure

If unsure of your immunity, visit a travel clinic 2–4 weeks before departure to be assessed for one self-paid MMR dose.

Symptoms and warning signs

Measles starts with high fever, cough, runny nose and conjunctivitis; white Koplik spots may appear inside the cheeks.

After 3–4 days a red blotchy rash appears behind the ears and on the face and spreads downward over the body, fading after 4–7 days and leaving brown discolouration.

Warning signs — seek care immediately if any appear: Rapid or difficult breathing, persistent high fever, seizures, altered consciousness, severe diarrhoea with dehydration; infants, pregnant women and immunocompromised people are prone to pneumonia, ear infection and encephalitis.

How it spreads

Measles spreads through the air, droplets and direct contact with nose and throat secretions. About nine in ten non-immune people sharing a room with a patient will be infected; the virus can remain in the air for about 2 hours.

Patients are infectious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash appears.

Prevention

  • Children receive two doses of MMR: the first at 12 months and the second between age 5 and school entry.
  • Adults born in 1966 or later without measles immunity who plan to visit affected areas should see a travel clinic 2–4 weeks before departure to be assessed for one self-paid MMR dose; health workers and people in frequent contact with foreign nationals should also be assessed.
  • Infants under 1 year are not yet vaccinated; avoid taking them to affected areas unless necessary.
  • Affected areas follow Taiwan CDC travel health notice levels.

Diagnosis and treatment

There is no specific treatment. Care is supportive (fever control, fluids and nutrition) with treatment of complications such as ear infection or pneumonia. Patients are isolated until 4 days after rash onset.

Non-immune contacts can receive MMR within 72 hours of exposure, or immunoglobulin within 6 days as assessed by a doctor, to lower the risk or severity of illness.

Vaccine

MMR (measles, mumps and rubella) is a live attenuated vaccine; two doses are about 97% effective. It must not be given during pregnancy or to severely immunocompromised people; women should avoid pregnancy for 4 weeks after vaccination. Adult recommendations follow the "Current MMR vaccination recommendations" revised on 16 April 2025.

Recent imported cases and related local clusters have been linked to outbreaks in Southeast Asia and Japan. See the dataset for annual counts.

Subsidies and services

Subsidies, applications and reporting services related to this disease.

  • Report a suspected outbreak (public, schools, facilities)
    If several people at the same place fall ill with similar symptoms within a short time, call 1922 or the local health bureau for investigation and testing.
  • Notifiable disease reporting (physicians and facilities)
    Physicians must report suspected notifiable diseases via NIDRS within category-specific deadlines (24 hours for categories 1–2, one week for most category 3).
  • Book a travel medicine clinic
    Book a contracted travel medicine clinic 4–6 weeks before departure for vaccines, malaria prophylaxis, the yellow card and pre-travel advice.
  • 預防接種紀錄與接種證明補發
    兒童預防接種紀錄卡遺失或入學、出國需要接種證明時，可至衛生所或原接種院所申請補發，也可透過健康存摺查詢下載接種紀錄。
