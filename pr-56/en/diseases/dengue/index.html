Dengue fever Category 2 notifiable disease

Dengue is caused by dengue viruses transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes. It does not spread directly from person to person. Taiwan is a high-risk area, with peaks in summer and autumn.

What to do

If youhave a fever and travelled to an affected area in the last two weeks

See a doctor within 24 hours

and mention your travel history; an NS1 rapid test may be done.

If youare confirmed and resting at home

The 1–2 days after the fever breaks are the most dangerous

Return immediately if any warning sign appears.

If youlive in an affected area

Check and empty standing water weekly

Wear light-coloured long sleeves and use repellent.

Symptoms and warning signs

Typical symptoms are sudden high fever (≥ 38°C), headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain and rash. Most people recover on their own.

A small number develop severe dengue, usually around the time the fever subsides. People infected a second time with a different serotype are at higher risk.

Warning signs — seek care immediately if any appear: Persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, mucosal bleeding, lethargy or restlessness, liver enlargement, rising haematocrit with falling platelets.

How it spreads

Dengue spreads through the bite of Aedes aegypti or Aedes albopictus mosquitoes carrying the virus. It is not transmitted directly between people. Patients are viraemic from one day before to five days after onset; a mosquito that bites them during this period can transmit the virus 8–12 days later.

Prevention

There is no routine vaccine in Taiwan. Prevention focuses on avoiding bites and removing breeding sites:

  • Check containers inside and outside your home every week: inspect, empty, clean and scrub.
  • Wear light-coloured long sleeves and trousers and use DEET, Picaridin or IR3535 repellent.
  • Install window and door screens; use bed nets if needed.
  • For two weeks after returning from an affected area, keep avoiding bites so you do not become a source of infection.

Diagnosis and treatment

There is no specific antiviral. Treatment is supportive: fluids and paracetamol for fever. Avoid aspirin and NSAIDs because of bleeding risk. During the febrile phase a doctor may order an NS1 antigen rapid test.

Vaccine

Dengue vaccine is not part of Taiwan's routine schedule. Any change will be announced officially by Taiwan CDC.

Subsidies and services

Subsidies, applications and reporting services related to this disease.

  • Report a suspected outbreak (public, schools, facilities)
    If several people at the same place fall ill with similar symptoms within a short time, call 1922 or the local health bureau for investigation and testing.
  • Laboratory testing for notifiable diseases (specimens from healthcare facilities)
    After notification, facilities collect specimens per lab specifications and send them with a requisition form; results are returned via NIDRS.
  • Notifiable disease reporting (physicians and facilities)
    Physicians must report suspected notifiable diseases via NIDRS within category-specific deadlines (24 hours for categories 1–2, one week for most category 3).
