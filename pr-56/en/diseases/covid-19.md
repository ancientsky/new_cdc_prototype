Most COVID-19 infections are mild, but older adults and high-risk groups can develop severe disease. Since March 2023 only "COVID-19 with severe complications" is notifiable (Category 4). Updated vaccines and early antivirals reduce severe illness.

## 一分鐘重點
- incubation: 2–14 days (mostly 2–4)
- symptoms: Fever, sore throat, cough, runny nose, fatigue, change in smell or taste
- transmission: Droplets, close-range airborne, contact
- prevention: Updated vaccine, mask when ill, hand washing
- notify: Severe COVID-19: per Category 4 rules

## What to do

- 如果你have fever, sore throat or similar symptoms：Wear a mask outside and avoid visiting older people; high-risk people should see a doctor early about treatment.
- 如果你are 65+ or have a chronic condition and test positive：A doctor can assess you for oral antivirals within 5 days of onset to reduce the risk of severe disease.
- 如果你have difficulty breathing, chest pain or confusion：These are signs of severe disease. Go to the emergency department or call 119.

## Symptoms and warning signs

Common symptoms include fever, sore throat, cough, runny nose, muscle aches, fatigue and headache; some people lose smell or taste or have diarrhoea. Most improve within about a week.

People aged 65+, those with chronic illness or weak immunity, pregnant women and the unvaccinated are more likely to develop pneumonia; some have lasting fatigue, breathlessness or brain fog ("long COVID").

**警示徵象：**Difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain or tightness, confusion or difficulty waking, blue lips or skin, inability to eat or drink, or markedly reduced urine.

## How it spreads

COVID-19 spreads mainly through **droplets** from coughing, sneezing and talking, and by inhaling virus-laden particles at close range in poorly ventilated spaces. Touching contaminated surfaces and then the face can also spread it.

People are most infectious from 1–2 days before to about 5–7 days after onset.

## Prevention

- **Get the latest COVID-19 vaccine**, especially if you are at high risk.
- Wear a mask and stay home when you have respiratory symptoms.
- Wash hands often and keep indoor spaces ventilated.
- Check that you have no symptoms before visiting hospitals, care homes or older relatives.

## Diagnosis and treatment

Most mild cases need only symptom relief. Eligible high-risk patients can receive **government-funded oral antivirals** (such as Paxlovid or molnupiravir) within 5 days of onset; hospitalised patients may receive remdesivir.

Some oral antivirals interact with common long-term medicines, so tell your doctor what you take.

## Vaccine

COVID-19 vaccines are updated as the virus changes and are offered each autumn alongside flu vaccine. **Everyone aged 6 months and over** can receive a government-funded dose; people aged 65+, chronic patients and health workers are priority groups. See the COVID-19 vaccine page.

## Current situation and statistics

Severe COVID-19 reports and outpatient visits are updated weekly.
