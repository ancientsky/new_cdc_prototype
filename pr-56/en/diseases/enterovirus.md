Enteroviruses are highly contagious and children under 5 are at greatest risk of severe illness. Most cases present as hand, foot and mouth disease or herpangina. Watch for warning signs such as lethargy or myoclonic jerks and go to a major hospital immediately.

## 一分鐘重點
- incubation: 2–10 days (average 3–5)
- symptoms: Fever, mouth ulcers, rash or blisters on hands, feet and buttocks
- transmission: Contact, droplets, faecal–oral
- prevention: Wash hands with soap, disinfect with bleach, keep sick children home
- notify: Severe complicated enterovirus infection: within one week

## What to do

- 如果你your child has hand, foot and mouth disease or herpangina：Keep the child home for at least 7 days, away from other children; wash hands with soap before and after care.
- 如果你there is lethargy, myoclonic jerks or persistent vomiting：Go immediately to a designated severe-enterovirus hospital or a large hospital with paediatric emergency care.
- 如果你there are children under 5 at home：Adults should wash hands with soap and change out of outdoor clothes before holding young children.

## Symptoms and warning signs

Most infections are mild and appear in two common forms:

- **Hand, foot and mouth disease**: fever, ulcers in the mouth and on the tongue, and red spots or small blisters on the palms, soles, knees and buttocks.
- **Herpangina**: sudden high fever with small blisters or ulcers at the back of the throat; children may refuse food and drool.

Most recover within 7–10 days, but a few develop encephalitis, myocarditis or acute flaccid paralysis; children under 5 are at highest risk.

**警示徵象：**Warning signs: drowsiness, reduced consciousness, poor activity, limb weakness, myoclonic jerks (sudden startles or whole-body muscle jerks), persistent vomiting, rapid breathing or rapid heartbeat.

## How it spreads

Enteroviruses spread through **contact** with saliva, nasal secretions, blister fluid or stool, through **droplets** from coughs and sneezes, and through contaminated hands, toys or food.

People are most contagious from a few days before to one week after onset, and the virus can be shed in stool for weeks, so hand washing after using the toilet remains important.

## Prevention

- **Wash hands properly with soap and water**; alcohol hand rub is less effective against enteroviruses.
- Wipe surfaces and toys with **500 ppm bleach** (1 part household bleach to 100 parts water).
- Keep sick children at home and away from other children.
- Childcare settings should inform parents and step up cleaning when a case occurs; classes may be suspended under local rules when 2 or more children in a class fall ill within a week.

## Diagnosis and treatment

There is no specific medicine. Care is supportive: fluids, fever control and pain relief for mouth ulcers; cool, soft food helps.

Children with warning signs should be transferred quickly to a **designated severe-enterovirus hospital**, where doctors may consider immunoglobulin, inotropes or ECMO.

## Vaccine

Licensed **enterovirus A71 (EV71) vaccines** are available in Taiwan on a self-paid basis; eligible ages follow each product label (mostly 2 months to under 6 years). The vaccine protects only against EV71, so hand washing is still essential.

## Current situation and statistics

Weekly enterovirus outpatient and emergency visits are updated with an epidemic threshold indicator; cases rise after school starts and in April to June.
