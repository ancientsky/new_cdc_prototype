Mpox Category 2 notifiable disease

Mpox (formerly monkeypox) spreads mainly through close contact and causes fever, swollen lymph nodes and rash. High-risk groups can get two government-funded doses of mpox vaccine. It has been a Category 2 notifiable disease since 2023.

What to do

If youhave an unexplained rash or blisters after close contact in the past 3 weeks

Cover the rash and see a doctor

Wear a mask and long sleeves, see a doctor and mention your contact history; designated friendly clinics are available in each city and county.

If youhad close contact with a confirmed case

Get assessed for vaccination within 4 days

Vaccination works best within 4 days of exposure and no later than 14 days; monitor your health for 21 days.

If youhave had multiple sexual partners or similar risks in the past year

Get two government-funded doses

Eligible people can book the government-funded vaccine; full protection requires two doses at least 4 weeks apart.

Symptoms and warning signs

Early symptoms include fever, headache, muscle and back aches, fatigue and swollen lymph nodes.

After 1–3 days a rash appears and progresses from spots to bumps, blisters, pustules and scabs. It may concentrate on the genitals, anus, mouth, face, hands or feet and can be painful or itchy. Illness lasts 2–4 weeks and most people recover on their own.

Warning signs — seek care immediately if any appear: Widespread lesions or lesions in the eye, rectal pain or bleeding, difficulty swallowing, altered consciousness; people with weakened immunity (such as uncontrolled HIV) are at higher risk of severe disease.

How it spreads

Mpox spreads mainly through direct contact with rash, scabs or body fluids; recent outbreaks are linked to sexual and other intimate contact. It can also spread through prolonged face-to-face droplets, contaminated bedding, clothing or towels, and from mother to fetus.

People are infectious from symptom onset until all scabs have fallen off and new skin has formed.

Prevention

  • Eligible high-risk groups should get two government-funded doses of mpox vaccine.
  • Avoid intimate contact with unknown partners; condoms do not fully prevent mpox but reduce other STI risks.
  • Do not share towels, bedding, clothes or utensils.
  • When visiting affected countries, avoid contact with wild animals, especially rodents.

Diagnosis and treatment

Most patients receive supportive care: pain relief, keeping lesions clean and dry and preventing bacterial infection. Severe or high-risk patients may be assessed for the antiviral tecovirimat.

Patients should avoid close contact with others until all lesions have scabbed over and fallen off.

Vaccine

Taiwan uses a non-replicating live vaccine (JYNNEOS), two doses at least 4 weeks apart. Government-funded groups include people with high-risk sexual behaviour in the past year (such as multiple partners, sex workers, or sex at commercial venues) and contacts assessed by health authorities after exposure.

Current situation and statistics

Cases in Taiwan are sporadic. Clade I outbreaks are concentrated in parts of central and east Africa; see the travel section for notice levels.
